# Dieter Rams scorecard

1. **Good design is innovative — Score: 2/3**  
   Evidence: The browser-only local file/folder workflow, restoration, three views, and lazy Excalidraw integration refresh familiar editor patterns (`01-evidence.md#1-innovative`).  
   Justification: The combination clearly improves the browser-editor pattern, but does not introduce an interaction proven unique across five or more peers.

2. **Good design makes a product useful — Score: 2/3**  
   Evidence: Core file and editing actions are directly available, while reordering and resizing remain pointer-only (`01-evidence.md#2-useful`).  
   Justification: The primary open-edit-save task is short and direct, but accessibility gaps add detours for some users.

3. **Good design is aesthetic — Score: 2/3**  
   Evidence: The neutral color, compact spacing, and aligned chrome form a consistent system, but two enabled text cues fail AA contrast (`01-evidence.md#3-aesthetic`).  
   Justification: The surface has a strong visible system with a small number of consequential token inconsistencies.

4. **Good design makes a product understandable — Score: 1/3**  
   Evidence: Primary verbs and semantics are clear, but Markdown, Open Folder, Upload image or file, Documents, the dirty bullet, and Not saved require interpretation or misstate behavior (`01-evidence.md#4-understandable`).  
   Justification: More than three user-facing labels or cues are unclear or mismatched, so the lower anchor applies.

5. **Good design is unobtrusive — Score: 2/3**  
   Evidence: Content owns most of the viewport and the chrome is quiet, although 25 persistent formatting commands create density (`01-evidence.md#5-unobtrusive`).  
   Justification: Chrome remains subordinate to content, but is too continuously visible to fully recede.

6. **Good design is honest — Score: 2/3**  
   Evidence: There are no dark patterns or promotional claims; several labels have minor behavior/scope mismatches (`01-evidence.md#6-honest`).  
   Justification: The product is fundamentally candid, but the label-to-behavior mapping is not fully one-to-one.

7. **Good design is long-lasting — Score: 3/3**  
   Evidence: System typography, neutral tokens, and familiar editor geometry avoid dated trend markers (`01-evidence.md#7-long-lasting`).  
   Justification: The visual language is restrained and likely to remain current for at least three years.

8. **Good design is thorough down to the last detail — Score: 2/3**  
   Evidence: All six required states exist, but contrast, skip-navigation, pointer-only interactions, and small targets remain rough (`01-evidence.md#8-thorough`).  
   Justification: State coverage is complete, yet one broad detail category—accessibility polish—needs refinement.

9. **Good design is environmentally friendly — Score: 1/3**  
   Evidence: Initial observed Vditor JavaScript is about 970KB compressed; motion is restrained and gated, Excalidraw is lazy, but dark mode is not automatically honored (`01-evidence.md#9-environmentally-friendly`).  
   Justification: The 500KB–2MB initial-JS anchor applies despite otherwise responsible motion and lazy loading.

10. **Good design is as little design as possible — Score: 1/3**  
    Evidence: The initial surface has 43 visible interactives, including 25 persistent toolbar commands and several advanced actions (`01-evidence.md#10-as-little-design-as-possible`).  
    Justification: More than five default-surface elements could be deferred without breaking the primary task.

**Total: 18/30**
